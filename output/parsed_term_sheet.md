# New York State Housing Finance Agency
## Affordable Rental Housing
### 4% Stand-alone LIHTC Term Sheet &#x26; Financing Guide

----

### PROGRAM DESCRIPTION

**General**
The New York State Housing Finance Agency (HFA or the Agency) is authorized to provide Federal 4% Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (4% LIHTC) allowable to projects financed by Private Activity Bonds issued by other agencies, such as Industrial Development Agencies and Public Housing Authorities. HFA maintains a separate application process for such tax-exempt bond financed projects (also referred to as 4% Stand-alone LIHTC projects), which only require HFA’s provision 4% LIHTC.

HFA’s issuance of tax credits for 4% Stand-alone LIHTC projects is made in accordance with applicable Federal and State law and regulations, including but not limited to Section 42 of the Internal Revenue Code (the Code) and the Agency’s Qualified Allocation Plan (the QAP). 4% Stand-alone LIHTC projects must adhere to HFA’s requirements and standards, as set forth herein, in the QAP and HFA’s 4% LIHTC Regulatory Agreement, as well as other Agency guidance. Such projects will be subject to HFA’s compliance monitoring through the regulatory term.

----

### Eligible Uses
- New construction of multifamily rental housing, including the creation of new residential units through the adaptive reuse of existing non-residential structures.

----

### Affordability Requirements
The project must meet one of the following LIHTC minimum low-income set aside requirements:
1. At least 20% of all the units reserved for very low-income households earning at or less than 50% AMI; or
2. At least 40% (25% in New York City) of the units set aside for households earning at or less than 60% AMI.
3. Projects proposing the LIHTC Average Income set aside must meet the following statutory parameters:
- a) At least 40% (or 25% for projects located in New York City) of the project units are both rent-restricted and occupied by individuals who do not exceed the imputed income limitations designated by the project owner;
- b) The average imputed income limitations designated cannot exceed 60% AMI; and,
- c) The designated imputed income limitations must be in 10% increments ranging from 20% to 80% AMI.

----

### Rent Limits
For all income-restricted units, gross rents cannot exceed 30% of the applicable percentage of AMI adjusted for family size. In some cases, HFA may allow for some flexibility within an income band if required by other funders of the project to accommodate specific public policy goals. For example, maximum rents set at 57% of AMI may be permitted for a unit restricted for households earning up to 60% of AMI.

----

| Requirement | Description |

| Waiver | The Regulatory Agreement will require that the project owner waive the ability to request a Qualified Contract to terminate the Extended Use period under §42(h)(6)(E) of the Code. |
| Execution | The Regulatory Agreement must be executed prior to the issuance of the tax-exempt bonds. |

## Development Team Requirements
| Eligible Applicants | Not-for-profit developers, for-profit developers, individuals, corporations, limited partnerships, and limited liability corporations. |

## Developer and Agency Fees
| Fee Type | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Developer’s Fee | The maximum allowable developer fee is not to exceed 10% of acquisition plus 15% on all other project costs, excluding reserves, partnership expenses, syndication fees, and developer fee. Developer fees may be reduced and are subject to Agency review. |
| Deferred Developer Fee | Deferred developer fee is considered an equity contribution and a financing source for the project. Deferred fee at permanent financing closing must be the aggregate amount that can be repaid by the projected cash flow as underwritten for the first 15 years after conversion. |
| Agency Fees | HFA requires an application fee of $3,000 and a credit issuance fee of eight percent (8%) of the first-year credit amount, due prior to HFA’s issuance of IRS Form(s) 8609 evidencing the final credit amount. Annual monitoring fees will be set at one percent (1%) of the maximum restricted rents for the low-income units with a minimum annual monitoring fee of $10,000. These fees are subject to revision by the Agency without prior notice and will be adjusted, if necessary, to reflect the cost to the Agency of the application, 8609 issuance, and compliance monitoring processes. |

# 4% Stand-alone LIHTC Term Sheet and Financing Guide

## REVIEWS AND APPROVALS

### Environmental
- Must respond to all requests in Environmental Unit’s “Follow-up Letter” (FUL) and achieve clearance from the HCR Environmental Unit. An applicant must complete the following at application. Later in the development process, additional items may be required by the Environmental Unit, per the Design Guidelines.
1. File an application with the New York State Office of Parks Recreation and Historic Preservation’s (NYS OPRHP) Cultural Resource Information System.
2. Prepare a Phase I Environmental Site Assessment (ESA) Report. The Phase I ESA must be submitted with the initial application and must meet the standards set forth in HCR’s current Design Guidelines. If recommended by the Phase I ESA or otherwise required, additional studies and mitigation plans shall be submitted prior to project clearance.
3. If the Project involves federal funding and/or permits, a National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) federal environmental review will be required, in addition to the above requirements. If project funding sources, such as HOME or CDBG funds, or Project Based Vouchers are utilized, this may trigger NEPA; a review process which might add time to the project’s development process.

- The Short Environmental Assessment Form-Part 1 (SEAF-Part 1): Certain projects may require completion of a Full Environmental Assessment Form- Part 1. The applicant will be notified by the Agency’s Environmental Unit if that is the case.

### Appraisal Report
- An independent, third-party appraisal conducted by a New York State licensed appraiser, and commissioned by the credit enhancement provider is required and subject to HFA review and approval. The project’s acquisition cost as set forth in the appraisal must be the lesser of the actual acquisition cost or the value supported by an as-is value and should distinctly separate the value attributed to the land allowing for a clear assessment of the land’s contribution to the overall cost. The appraisal must be consistent with the analysis of rents and operating expenses presented in the application. If necessary, an independent market study may be requested.

### Scope of Work
- All projects must comply with the HCR’s Design Guidelines effective as of the date of the application submission. All projects will be required to submit an Architect’s Certificate from a New York State Registered Architect, certifying that the project will comply with the current HCR Design Guidelines.

- Plans must be prepared by an architectural firm acceptable to HFA. Architects are responsible for providing designs that are at a minimum based on the applicable codes and regulations. Plans will be reviewed by HCR Design, Construction &#x26; Environmental Unit staff for compliance with applicable codes, regulations, policies, and overall appropriateness to serve the intended population(s).

# 4% Stand-alone LIHTC Term Sheet and Financing Guide

## Key Guidelines

### HCR Project Review
- HCR reserves the right to reject projects that exceed the QAP’s per unit eligible basis limit or have per unit development costs that exceed reasonable costs based on agency experience in the applicable region.

### Sustainability Guidelines
- All projects must comply with HCR’s Sustainability Guidelines effective as of the application submission date.
- An Architect’s Certificate from a New York State Registered Architect is required to certify compliance with HCR’s Sustainability Guidelines.

### Subsidy Layering Review
- A Subsidy Layering Review (SLR) is required for all projects receiving Federal government subsidies or HUD assistance to ensure no excessive public funds are received.

## Application Process
- All applications must be fully complete and submitted to HFA no later than **October 1st** to meet a year-end closing timeframe.
- Applications submitted after October 1st will not be reviewed until the following calendar year.
- Applicants must submit a **4% Stand-alone LIHTC Application** for review and approval by HFA. The application can be found here.
- HFA reserves the right to request additional information for project review and determination of 4% LIHTC issuance.
